1. SETUP NAME
"Range-floor rebound with positive factor alpha". A small, conditional long that the stock bounces off the bottom of its 60-day range. It is a marginal setup, not a strong one (see section 10).

2. WHAT MUST BE TRUE FOR ENTRY, AND WHETHER IT IS TRUE AT THE DAY 0 CLOSE
The thresholds are my own choices (analysis). The values come from the cited lines.
  a. Day 0 close is no more than 3% above the 12-month closing low and not below it.
     Close 100.00 vs 252d low 98.79, which is +1.2% (RH). TRUE.
  b. RSI is below 40 (weak, but not a free fall below 30 that I would treat as a separate case).
     RSI 35.5 (RH). TRUE.
  c. No volume spike: 5-day volume at most 1.5x normal.
     5d volume 1.01x (RH). TRUE.
  d. The factor model still ranks the stock among the names the phase 8 baseline would hold (top 5 of 24 by alpha t-stat).
     Rank 3 of 24, alpha 21.5% a year, t-stat 1.24 (SC). TRUE.
  e. No Model 4 supply shock in the 120 days before Day 0 that names this stock's group as an expected loser.
     "None" (SC; NH). TRUE.
Result: every condition is true at the Day 0 close. Nothing in the plan depends on Day 1 or later prices.

3. ENTRY RULE
If conditions a-e hold at the Day 0 close, BUY at the Day 1 close, whatever that price is (FR). Entry = the Day 1 fill price. That price is MISSING at decision time, so stop and target are set as percents of the fill, not as fixed price levels.

4. STOP RULE
Exit at the first daily close 8% or more below entry (FR: maximum stop 15%; checked on closes only).
Why 8% (analysis): 20-day volatility is 37.7% a year (RH). Over one day that is about 37.7% / sqrt(252) = 37.7 / 15.87 = 2.4%. A stop just under the 98.79 support (about 2% below the Day 0 close) would be hit by a single normal day's move. 8% is about 3.4 daily moves (8 / 2.4 = 3.3), which leaves room for noise. If the Day 1 fill is near 100, an 8% stop sits near 92, clearly below the 98.79 floor, so it also says "the floor failed".

5. TARGET / EXIT RULE AND HOLDING PERIOD
Take profit at the first daily close 14% or more above entry. If neither stop nor target is hit, close at the close of trading day 20 after entry (FR maximum).
Why 14% (analysis): the next clear resistance above the 107.1-107.6 zone is the 60-day/20-day highs of 113.36-114.20 (RH), about +13.4% to +14.2% from a 100 fill. The nearer 107.1-107.6 zone (+7.1% to +7.6%) is a likely pause point and the main obstacle to the target.
For scale (analysis): 20-day volatility over 20 trading days is about 37.7% x sqrt(20/252) = 37.7% x 0.282 = 10.6%, so a 14% move in 20 days is larger than one typical 20-day swing.

6. REWARD-TO-RISK RATIO
Gross: 14% / 8% = 1.75.
With costs (FR: 0.1% fees + 0.1% slippage on each side = 0.2% per side, 0.4% round trip, my approximation applied as simple percents):
  net reward = 14% - 0.4% = 13.6%
  net risk = 8% + 0.4% = 8.4%
  13.6 / 8.4 = 1.62.
Because stops are checked on closes only, a real exit could be worse than -8% if the stock gaps down; the 8.4% risk figure is a floor, not a cap.

7. POSITION SIZE
5% of the simulated portfolio = 0.05 x $100,000 = $5,000 (FR maximum is 10%).
Reason for half size (analysis): the downtrend, the unexplained drop and the missing inputs below.
Planned loss if the stop is hit at exactly -8.4% net: $5,000 x 0.084 = $420, about 0.42% of the portfolio. This could be larger after a gap.

8. WHAT WOULD CANCEL THE SETUP
Before entry (all judged at the Day 0 close, the only point a decision can use):

  - The Risk Manager finds the Day 0 data unreliable (for example, the Day -13 volume reading of 0.00x turns out to mean other bad rows).
  - Any confirmed negative company-specific news before the Day 0 close (none is supplied; headlines N/A BY DESIGN).
After entry, the position closes only by its rules: stop, target, or day 20. Do not change them along the way.

10. REASONS TO SKIP IT
  - Trend is against the trade: close below the 20d, 50d and 200d averages, the 200d is 23.5% above price, and returns are negative in every window (RH). Buying near a low in a downtrend often fails.
  - The stock trails the chip ETF in every window, by 26.8 points over 12 months (RH), and the cause is unknown (NH). An unseen negative catalyst cannot be ruled out.
  - The 98.79 floor is one day old and untested (RH). It is a closing low, not a proven support level.
  - The factor alpha is not statistically strong: t-stat 1.24 is well under the usual ~2 (SC). Rank 3 of 24 is relative, and the regression uses 156 weeks that mostly come before the recent fall. Down-week beta to the ETF (1.03) is higher than up-week beta (0.84) (SC), so the stock has tended to fall with the sector more than it rises with it.
  - The 14% target needs a move bigger than one typical 20-day swing (10.6%, analysis) and has to pass the 107.1-107.6 resistance first.
  - I set the entry thresholds after seeing the data, so they may simply describe today's numbers instead of testing an edge.
If the Risk Manager or the Boss weighs these as heavier than the rebound case, NO TRADE is a reasonable outcome. This plan is not safe, profitable or high-probability; it is a defined-risk test case for the simulation.
