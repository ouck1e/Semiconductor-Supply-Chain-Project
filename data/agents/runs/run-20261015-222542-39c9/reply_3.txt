Simulation for research and learning only. This is not investment advice and promises nothing about profit. No real order is placed. The Risk Manager and the Boss decide whether a simulated trade happens.

1. SETUP NAME
"Uptrend pullback to the 20-day average, inside a low-volume range" (conditional, long-only).

2. WHAT MUST BE TRUE FOR ENTRY, AND WHETHER IT IS TRUE ON DAY 0
All figures come from the Researcher handoff unless I say otherwise.
a) Longer-term uptrend: close above the 50-day and 200-day averages, with the averages stacked.
   Source: close 100.00; averages 99.91 / 92.06 / 83.01; the Researcher calls them "stacked rising averages". TRUE.
b) Price has pulled back to short-term support but not broken it: close within about 1% of the 20-day average and at or above the range floor of 98.48.
   Source: close is +0.1% vs the 20-day average; the range is 98.48-105.17. TRUE (100.00 >= 98.48).
c) Momentum not overextended: RSI between 40 and 65.
   Source: RSI 54.9. TRUE.
d) The pullback is not on heavy selling: Day 0 volume ratio below 1.0x.
   Source: Day 0 fell 2.8% on 0.88x volume. TRUE.
e) No listed supply shock against the stock.
   Source: News Analyst handoff and Model 4, "None." TRUE (but "None" only covers the events the model tracks).
f) Supporting, not required: Model 1 alpha t-stat 1.80, rank 1 of 24. That puts the stock inside the phase 8 baseline's top 5.
   Source: supply-chain model facts. Present, but a t-stat of 1.80 is below the usual 2.0 bar, so I treat it as weak support.
Result: every required condition (a-e) holds at the Day 0 close.

3. ENTRY RULE
At the Day 0 close, place a simulated BUY that fills at the Day 1 close. Entry price = Day 1 close, which is MISSING (it is in the future). Fees of 0.1% and slippage of 0.1% apply on entry.
Fill condition (see item 8): skip the fill if the Day 1 close is below 98.48, or more than 3% above the Day 0 close (above 103.00). If the simulator cannot do conditional fills, the Risk Manager should know the BUY would fill at the Day 1 close whatever it is.

4. STOP RULE
Stop at 10% below the entry price, checked on daily closes only.
Why 10%: it puts the stop below the 20-day low (90.60, -9.4% from 100.00) and the 50-day average (92.06, -7.9%). A tighter stop just under the 98.48 range floor would be only about 1.5% away, which is less than one typical day's move at 41.9% annualized volatility (about 2.6% a day, my arithmetic: 41.9% / sqrt(252)). That stop would likely be hit by ordinary noise.
10% is within the 15% maximum. Because checks use closes only, a gap down could close well past the stop. The 2.51 down-week beta to the chip ETF makes that more likely.

5. TARGET / EXIT RULE AND MAXIMUM HOLDING PERIOD
Target at 18% above entry, checked on daily closes. If entry is near 100.00, that is about 118, just under the 252-day high of 118.29 (Researcher's second resistance level).
Time exit: close at the Day 20 close (counting trading days from the Day 1 fill) if neither stop nor target has been hit. That is the 20-day maximum.
Note: the first resistance, 105.17, is only about +5.2% from 100.00. Price may stall there. For a sense of scale: over 20 days, 41.9% annualized volatility is roughly 11.8% (my arithmetic: 41.9% x sqrt(20/252)). So an 18% move in 20 days is possible but well beyond one standard move. The time exit may be the most likely outcome.

6. REWARD-TO-RISK RATIO
Gross: target % / stop % = 18 / 10 = 1.8.
After costs (0.2% per side x 2 sides = 0.4% round trip): reward about 18 - 0.4 = 17.6; risk about 10 + 0.4 = 10.4; 17.6 / 10.4 = about 1.69.

7. POSITION SIZE
5% of the simulated portfolio = $5,000 (my proposal; the cap is 10%).
Loss if stopped at exactly -10%, plus 0.4% costs: $5,000 x 10.4% = $520, about 0.52% of the portfolio. A gap through the stop would lose more.
Why half the cap: market beta 1.99, down-week ETF beta 2.51, no news context (blind run), and the alpha t-stat is below 2.0. At the full 10% cap the stop loss would be about $1,040 (1.04%). The Risk Manager can choose between the two.

8. WHAT WOULD CANCEL THE SETUP
- Day 1 close below 98.48: the range floor has broken, so do not fill.
- Day 1 close above 103.00 (more than 3% above Day 0): the entry is no longer a pullback, and there is less room to first resistance. Do not fill.
- Any news or scheduled event (for example earnings) that appears before the fill. None is supplied; such events are MISSING.
- A Model 4 supply shock listed against this stock's group before the fill.
- The Risk Manager finding a data problem, for example the ETF 6m and 12m returns both being 27.3%.

9. MISSING INPUTS
- Day 1 close (the entry price) and every later price.
- All headlines (withheld by design); earnings dates; fundamentals.
- Why the heavy-volume up moves on Days -26, -24 and -15 happened.
- The full 252-day close series and daily ETF prices; absolute volumes.
- Dates of the 60-day low and the 252-day high and low.
- Whether the simulator supports conditional fills on Day 1.
- Supply exposure for helium from Qatar covers only 16% of capacity, so that figure is incomplete.

10. REASONS TO SKIP IT
- Blind run: no news, so the setup rests on technicals plus a weak-ish factor alpha.
- Stock A is behind the chip ETF on 1m (7.7% vs 11.7%) and 6m (10.5% vs 27.3%). That is relative weakness lately.
- Day 0 was a -2.8% day; the pullback may not be finished.
- High beta (1.99 market; 2.51 to the ETF in down weeks). A sector selloff would likely hit this stock harder, and close-only stops can gap.
- The 12m return of 174.1% is extended. Price is 15.5% below the 252-day high, and the 120-day volatility (70.9%) shows big recent swings.
- Alpha t-stat 1.80 is not significant at the usual level. The factor data is lagged 61 days.
- The supply exposure is concentrated, for example gallium from China / Hong Kong SAR 0.574 and rare gas from USA 0.639. Model 4 lists no shock, but if one hit, the stock's share of exposure is large.
- The 18% target is ambitious for 20 days relative to volatility. The net reward-to-risk (about 1.69) is modest.
- A NO TRADE decision is a reasonable outcome.
