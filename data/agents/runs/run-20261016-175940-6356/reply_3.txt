3. Entry rule
If conditions a-e hold at the Day 0 close (they do), BUY at the Day 1 close, whatever that price is. No condition depends on Day 1 or later. The stop and target below are percentages of the actual Day 1 fill. If Day 1 fills far from 100, the price levels they map to move with it.

4. Stop rule
Exit at the first daily close 6.0% or more below entry. If the fill is near 100.00, the stop is about 94.0. That sits just under the 94.82 higher low (Researcher support), so a close there would break the base structure. The stop is wider than the swing low on purpose: 20-day volatility of 47.8% works out to roughly 3% a day (my own arithmetic: 47.8 / sqrt(252) ≈ 3.0), so a stop placed right at 94.82 would sit inside ordinary noise.

5. Target or exit rule
Exit at the first daily close 10.5% or more above entry, about 110.5 if the fill is near 100.00. Otherwise exit at the close of trading day 20 after entry (the maximum hold). Note that the target sits about 1.3% above the 108.25-109.04 resistance cluster (Researcher). The stock has to clear that cluster on a closing basis to get there.

6. Reward-to-risk
Round-trip costs = 2 x (0.1% fee + 0.1% slippage) = 0.4% (fixed rules).
Before costs: 10.5 ÷ 6.0 = 1.75.
After costs: (10.5 − 0.4) ÷ (6.0 + 0.4) = 10.1 ÷ 6.4 = 1.58. That clears the 1.5 minimum, but not by much.
Why not a pair that sits on the levels exactly: a target at the 108.25 resistance (+8.25%) with a stop just under 94.82 (about −5.5%) gives (8.25 − 0.4) ÷ (5.5 + 0.4) = 7.85 ÷ 5.9 = 1.33, which fails. A stop under the 90.45 floor (about −10%) would need a target of at least +16% (since (16 − 0.4) ÷ 10.4 = 1.5), which runs well past the 108-109 cluster. The only pair that passes asks for a modest close beyond resistance.

7. Position size
5% of the simulated portfolio ($5,000 of $100,000). This is half the 10% maximum because the trend is down, volatility is high, market beta is 1.31, and down-week beta (1.24) is higher than up-week beta (0.93) (model facts). Loss if stopped near the stop level: about 5% x 6.4% ≈ 0.32% of the portfolio, before any gap past the stop on a close.
